Reading the five-station comparison

2014 and 2015 are the first two full calendar years with at least 325 valid days at every U.S. post. Both years follow the plan. Annual means weight valid days equally; missing days are excluded. Cities are sorted by percentage change. The WHO 2021 annual reference of 5 µg/m³ is retrospective.

Three station means fell and two rose slightly. These are descriptive changes, without a significance test. Regional targets use a different spatial scope; a consulate reading cannot establish that a city or region met its target.

What the extreme-day comparison shows

Switch between observed day counts and their share of valid days. Shares use valid observed days, not all calendar days. Missing observations are excluded rather than counted as clean days, and different coverage can affect both measures. The threshold of 150 µg/m³ is a concentration threshold, not an AQI category.

The complication · Beijing

What if the wind changed the picture?

Beijing: 2011–2012 versus 2014–2015, leaving 2013 as a transition year. Does the change look the same under different winds?

Same city. Different winds.

How to read the wind check

Wind direction is one part of the weather. The charts compare 2011–2012 to 2014–2015 and omit 2013 as a transition year. Dominant wind uses the most frequent recorded category among valid PM hours; at least 18 known directions are required. Ties are excluded.

Matching calendar-month and wind groups does not control for temperature, humidity, rain, wind speed, changes in economic activity, or emissions. This is a sensitivity check, not a causal estimate of policy impact.

What the findings mean

Three lessons for Thailand

Measure first. Publish reliable readings and missing-data coverage.

Compare like with like. Account for weather and seasonal differences before attributing changes to policy.

Watch the worst days. Track extreme pollution as well as annual averages.

Sources & method

References

Measurements: UCI dataset 394 (Chen, 2016; CC BY 4.0), also distributed on Kaggle. Calculations use the original UCI download.

Research context: Liang et al. (2016), doi:10.1002/2016JD024877. Their full meteorological adjustment is not reproduced here.

Policy: State Council Action Plan, September 2013. Regional 2017 PM2.5 reduction goals versus 2012: Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei ~25%, Yangtze River Delta ~20%, Pearl River Delta ~15%; Beijing annual concentration ~60 µg/m³. These are not five identical city targets.

Health references: WHO 2021 guidelines and WHO 2005/2021 comparison table. Annual PM2.5: 10/5; 24-hour: 25/15 µg/m³. The 24-hour recommendation is evaluated at the annual 99th percentile.

Map: Natural Earth via world-atlas.

How the data are read

Aggregation: daily means require ≥18 valid hours; monthly means require ≥15 valid days; annual means weight all valid days equally. These are project completeness rules, not a claim of regulatory certification. No missing values are filled. Values are rounded for display only.

Sensitivity checks: the matched-calendar comparison uses only month/day pairs with valid readings in both 2014 and 2015. Beijing's month × wind check keeps groups with at least five eligible days per period, then applies identical weights to both periods: the average of each group's share of retained days in each period. Neither check isolates a policy effect.

Station comparison: both monitors must independently pass the daily completeness rule. Correlation, signed bias and MAE use all paired valid days. The duplicated Guangzhou column is excluded. File hashes and calculation inputs are included in the downloaded data.
